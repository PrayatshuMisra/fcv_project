Computer Vision Concepts & Parameter Engineering Manual

Foundations of Computer Vision (CSE 3176: Labs 1 to 5) • Clinical Dermatological Principles

Why this guide matters:

In computer vision and medical triage, parameter controls are not arbitrary levers. Every slider controls a specific mathematical transformation in the spatial, frequency, or color domain. This guide explains the exact mathematical operation, why it was chosen over alternatives, and what happens physically to image pixels when you increase or decrease each value.

Lab 1

Multi-Color Space Decomposition (RGB vs. CIE LAB vs. HSV)

Concept & Mathematical Foundation:

Standard RGB encodes images as red, green, and blue photon intensities. However, in RGB, chrominance (color) and luminance (lighting/brightness) are heavily coupled. If a clinical examination room's lighting changes, all three R, G, and B values shift unpredictably.

To solve this, we project pixels into CIE LAB and HSV color spaces:

  • CIE LAB L* (Lightness): Represents perceptual brightness (0 = black, 100 = diffuse white), completely decoupled from pigment color.
  • CIE LAB a* (Erythema / Redness Axis): Measures the green-to-red axis. In dermatology, elevated a* corresponds directly to vascular erythema, angiogenesis, and tumor blood vessel density.
  • CIE LAB b* (Melanin Accumulation Axis): Measures the blue-to-yellow axis. Corresponds directly to dermal melanin pigment clumping.
  • HSV Hue (H): The pure chromophore dominant wavelength angle (0° to 360°).
  • HSV Saturation (S): The purity or density of the chromophore (distinguishes dense melanin nests from fair skin).
Clinical Diagnostic Purpose:

By extracting a* and b*, the computer vision pipeline measures biological tumor markers (blood vessel growth and melanin density) without being tricked by room lighting, flash glares, or shadow gradients.

Lab 2

Spatial Domain Intensity Transformations & Dynamic Range Standardization

Algorithm Options & Working Principles:
  • CLAHE (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization): Subdivides the image into an 8 × 8 grid of contextual tiles. Equalizes the histogram within each tile while enforcing a strict clip limit (2.0) that redistributes excessive histogram peaks. Borders are seamlessly blended with bilinear interpolation.
  • Contrast Stretching (Min-Max Normalization): Linearly rescales pixel values across the full 8-bit dynamic range:
    Iout = [(I − Imin) / (Imax − Imin)] × 255
  • Global Histogram Equalization (GHE): Flattens the entire global cumulative distribution function (CDF).
  • Power-Law / Gamma Correction: Non-linear intensity mapping:
    s = c × rγ
↓ Decreasing Gamma (γ < 1.0)

Visual Impact: Non-linearly lifts and brightens mid-tones and dark shadows while compressing bright regions.

Clinical Utility: Uncovers dark, underexposed dermoscopic close-ups and reveals faint pigment networks previously buried in shadow.

↑ Increasing Gamma (γ > 1.0)

Visual Impact: Deepens shadows, darkens mid-tones, and steepens contrast transitions around boundaries.

Clinical Utility: Accentuates high-contrast melanoma borders against fair skin, but may obscure internal lesion details if set too high (>1.8).

Why CLAHE beats Global Equalization in Clinical Dermoscopy:

Healthy skin typically occupies 60%–80% of dermoscopy imagery. Global Histogram Equalization is dominated by this skin background, which over-amplifies uniform skin regions into washed-out, blown-out white blooming. CLAHE isolates each tile and prevents noise amplification, preserving razor-sharp lesion edges.

Lab 3

Artifact Suppression (DullRazor) & Spatial Domain Filtering

1. DullRazor Morphological Inpainting (Pre-Filter):

Terminal hair strands cross the skin and create false dark lines. DullRazor applies a Black Top-Hat transform with linear structuring elements:

Tb(f) = (f • B) − f
where • denotes morphological closing. This isolates dark, thin, directional lines while ignoring broad lesion pigmentation. Corrupted hair pixels are then reconstructed using Telea's Fast-Marching Inpainting, propagating surrounding epidermal texture along image gradients.

Clinical Impact: If hairs are not removed, Canny edge detection interprets hair as notched lesion boundaries, falsely inflating Border Irregularity (B) and corrupting K-Means clusters.

2. Spatial Noise Filters Compared:
  • Bilateral Filter (Recommended Clinical Standard): Non-linear, dual-Gaussian filter. Considers both spatial Euclidean distance (σs) and intensity photometric difference (σr).
    Why it is superior: Smooths dermal micro-pores and camera sensor noise across uniform skin, but when it encounters a steep lesion boundary, the photometric weight drops to 0, leaving the lesion border completely sharp and unblurred.
  • Gaussian Blur (Linear Low-Pass): Convolves an isotropic 2D Gaussian bell curve. Smooths noise but smears genuine malignant notched borders into soft gradients.
  • Median Filter (Order-Statistic): Replaces center pixel with the median of neighboring pixels. Ideal for suppressing impulse salt-and-pepper noise and fluid immersion bubbles.
↓ Decreasing Kernel Size (3 px – 5 px)

Visual Impact: Preserves ultra-fine textural details and delicate micro-vessel arborization.

Clinical Utility: Best for high-resolution dermoscopic imagery with low sensor noise.

↑ Increasing Kernel Size (7 px – 15 px)

Visual Impact: Aggressively attenuates intradermal texture, smoothing rough skin and noise specks.

Clinical Risk: Over-smoothing (>9px in Gaussian) can artificially round off malignant ragged edges, potentially causing false-negative border scores.

Lab 4

Boundary Delineation & Edge Operators (Canny vs. Sobel)

Canny 4-Stage Optimal Edge Detection:
  1. Gaussian Smoothing: Dampens high-frequency thermal sensor noise.
  2. Sobel Gradient Vector: Calculates directional derivatives Gx, Gy, gradient magnitude M = √(Gx2 + Gy2), and direction θ = arctan(Gy / Gx).
  3. Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS): Thinning process. A pixel is retained only if its gradient magnitude is a local maximum along the gradient direction θ, producing crisp 1-pixel-wide edge ridges.
  4. Hysteresis Thresholding (T₁, T₂): Dual thresholds prevent edge streaking and noise fragmentation.
↓ Lowering Lower Threshold (T₁) (e.g. 30 – 70)

Visual Impact: Accepts weaker gradient ridges and connects faint, low-contrast lesion boundaries.

Risk: May introduce spurious lines from normal skin wrinkles or follicular pits.

↑ Raising Upper Threshold (T₂) (e.g. 180 – 240)

Visual Impact: Demands steep intensity steps before initiating any edge contour.

Benefit: Guarantees that only definitive high-contrast lesion boundaries are registered.

Canny vs. Sobel in Lesion Diagnostics:

Sobel outputs a continuous, fuzzy gradient magnitude map showing edge strength everywhere. Canny applies Non-Maximum Suppression and Hysteresis to yield clean, single-pixel connected boundaries essential for mathematical contour tracking and geometric compactness calculations.

Lab 5

Unsupervised 3D K-Means Color Segmentation & Morphometrics

1. K-Means 3D Clustering:

Partitions pixels into K clusters by iteratively minimizing the sum of squared Euclidean distances in color space:

J = ∑j=1..K ∑x ∈ Sj ||x − μj||2
Each pixel is color-quantized to its nearest cluster centroid.

↓ Decreasing Clusters (K = 2 – 3)

Visual Impact: Forces high color aggregation. Produces clean binary partition: healthy epidermis vs. dark lesion parenchyma.

Clinical Utility: Optimal for overall lesion boundary delineation and perimeter tracking.

↑ Increasing Clusters (K = 4 – 6)

Visual Impact: Decomposes lesion into sub-compartments (black melanin core, light brown reticular network, erythema, hypopigmentation).

Clinical Utility: Essential for analyzing color variegation and intra-lesional heterogeneity.

2. Geometric Morphometrics & Overlay Primitives:
  • Primary Contour (Red): The actual boundary of the lesion, extracted via cv2.findContours. Gives exact perimeter P and area A.
  • Convex Hull Envelope (Cyan): The smallest convex polygon enclosing the lesion contour (cv2.convexHull).
  • Compactness Ratio:
    Compactness = P2 / (4 π × Area)
    A smooth circle has compactness ≈ 1.0. Notched, malignant lesions have high compactness (> 1.5).
  • Bounding Box (Green) & Centroid (Yellow Crosshair): Derived from spatial image moments M00, M10, M01 (cv2.moments). Provides the center of mass and principal inertia axes.
Lab 5

Quantitative Micro & Macro Texture Analysis (LBP & GLCM)

1. Local Binary Patterns (LBP) — Micro-Texture:

Encodes local micro-topography by thresholding a circular neighborhood of P points at radius R against the center pixel. We employ rotation-invariant Uniform LBP (patterns with ≤ 2 bitwise transitions).

↓ Decreasing Radius (R = 1 – 2)

Visual Impact: Samples immediate neighboring pixels within a tight micro-neighborhood.

Clinical Focus: Sensitive to fine dermal surface granularity, micro-pores, and fine pigment networks.

↑ Increasing Radius (R = 3 – 5)

Visual Impact: Expands circular sampling radius into surrounding tissue.

Clinical Focus: Captures macro-architectural textural patterns, pigment globules, blotches, and verrucous keratotic plaques.

2. Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM Haralick Descriptors) — Macro-Texture:

Computes second-order joint conditional probability distribution P(i, j | d, θ) of gray levels occurring at distance d ∈ {1, 3} across orientations θ ∈ {0°, 45°, 90°, 135°}.

  • Contrast: Measures local intensity variations. High in ulcerated, variegated, malignant tumors; low in uniform nevi.
  • Dissimilarity: Linear step variance between adjacent pixel pairs.
  • Homogeneity: Measures diagonal concentration. Elevated in smooth, uniform benign moles.
  • Energy / Angular Second Moment (ASM): Measures textural orderliness and uniformity.
  • Correlation: Measures linear gray-level dependency among adjacent pixels across spatial directions.
Clinical Triage

Stolz ABCD Rule & Total Dermoscopy Score (TDS) Clinical Protocol

The Mathematical Scoring Formula:
TDS = 1.3 × A + 0.1 × B + 0.5 × C + 0.5 × D
  • A — Asymmetry Score (0 to 2, Weight: 1.3): Evaluated by orienting the lesion along its two principal orthogonal inertia axes (major and minor) and folding the binary mask in half across each axis. If non-overlapping area exceeds 5%, asymmetry is flagged on that axis. Score: 0 = symmetric; 1 = 1-axis asymmetric; 2 = 2-axis asymmetric.
  • B — Border Irregularity (0 to 8, Weight: 0.1): The lesion is subdivided into 8 radial octants (45° wedges around the centroid). Each octant is evaluated for radial distance variance and sharp, abrupt border cutoffs vs. smooth gradual tapering. Each notched octant contributes 1 point.
  • C — Color Variegation (1 to 6, Weight: 0.5): Automatic chromophore detection for the 6 dermatological colors: 1. White (regression/hypopigmentation), 2. Red (erythema/vascularity), 3. Light Brown, 4. Dark Brown, 5. Slate Blue/Gray (deep dermal melanin), 6. Black (superficial melanin).
  • D — Diameter (1 to 5, Weight: 0.5): Maximum physical lesion dimension in millimeters. Clinically, lesions exceeding the >6 mm threshold carry elevated risk.
Calibrated Clinical Risk Stratification:
TDS < 4.75 Benign / Low Risk Routine annual dermatological surveillance recommended.
4.75 ≤ TDS < 5.45 Suspicious / Pre-Malignant Short-term digital dermoscopy monitoring (3 months) or punch biopsy indicated.
TDS ≥ 5.45 High Risk / Suggestive of Malignancy Urgent surgical referral and complete histopathological excision biopsy indicated.
